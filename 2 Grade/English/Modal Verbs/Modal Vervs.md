First, we use had to / didn’t have to to talk about necessity in the past. If something was necessary or unavoidable, we say had to. If something was not necessary, we use didn’t have to. This structure only describes the situation at that time; it does not tell us whether the action actually happened or not.


Next, to express regret or criticism about past actions, we use should (not) have or ought (not) to have + past participle. These forms are used when we look back and evaluate a situation. Should have means something would have been a better choice, while shouldn’t have shows that something was a mistake.


Finally, we use didn’t need to + verb and needn’t have + past participle to talk about unnecessary actions, but with an important difference. Didn’t need to + verb means the action was unnecessary and did not happen. In contrast, needn’t have + past participle means the action was unnecessary, but it was done anyway.
