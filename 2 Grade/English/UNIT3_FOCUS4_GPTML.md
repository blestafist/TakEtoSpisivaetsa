1. Quantifiers: Both, Each, Every, and All
Functional Rules
 * Both: Used for exactly 2 things or people.
   * Structure: both + noun, both of the + noun, both of them.
   * Examples: both places, both of the places, both of them.
 * Each: Used for 2 or more items when emphasizing individuals separately.
   * Structure: each + singular noun, each (one) of the + plural noun, each (one) of them.
   * Examples: each hole, each (one) of the Hobbit holes, each (one) of them.
   * Context: Each Hobbit hole had a slightly different door and I took a selfie in front of each one.
 * Every: Used for 3 or more items when thinking of them as a group.
   * Structure: every + singular noun, every (one) of the + plural noun, every one of them.
   * Examples: every Hobbit movie, every one of the Hobbit movies, every one of them.
 * All: Used for 3 or more items (the whole group).
   * Structure: all the + plural noun, all (of) the + plural noun, all of them.
   * Examples: all the tourists, all (of) the tourists, all of them.
Specific Usage Notes
 * Adverbs: Use every or all (NOT each) with adverbs like almost, nearly, practically.
   * Example: I've visited practically every country in Asia.
 * All vs. Whole: Both talk about 100%. Use all (of) the or the whole (of) the. Do not use the whole with plural nouns.
   * Examples: all the tourists (NOT the whole tourists); all the time / the whole time; all the family / the whole family.
2. Distributives & Reciprocals
 * Another: Means 'a different' or 'an additional'. Used with singular nouns.
   * Example: another cookie.
 * Other: Means 'different' ones. Used with plural nouns.
   * Example: other drinks.
 * The other(s): Refers to the second of two or the rest of a group.
   * Examples: I'll carry this bag. Can you carry the other one?, I can see Tom. Where are the others?
 * Each other / One another: Used for reciprocal actions (same meaning).
   * Example: My sister and I took photos of each other. (Meaning: She took a photo of me and I took a photo of her).

|---|---|---|
| Future Continuous | Unfinished actions in progress at a specific future time. | At 11 a.m. tomorrow, I'll be doing an exam. |
| Future Perfect Simple | Actions completed before a certain time in the future. | By 11 a.m. tomorrow, I'll have done an exam. |

3. Expressing Concession
 * Rule A: Although / Even though + clause (subject + verb).
   * Examples:
     * Although halls of residence are not always the cheapest option, many students prefer them.
     * Many students prefer halls of residence even though they are not always the cheapest option.
     * Although there are numerous arguments for living with your parents while at university, there are also considerable drawbacks.
 * Rule B: In spite of / Despite + noun or -ing.
   * Examples:
     * In spite of the cost, many students prefer halls of residence.
     * Many students prefer halls of residence despite the cost.
     * Despite living cheaply, many students still find themselves short of money.
     * Despite the numerous arguments for living with your parents while at university, there are also considerable drawbacks.
5. Writing Focus: 'For and Against' Essay
Structure & Phrases
 * Introduction: State the topic generally and mention both sides.
   * General: Many young people beginning university choose to live in halls of residence.
   * Thesis: Although there are several potential downsides to..., there are also numerous benefits.
 * Main Paragraphs: Present arguments clearly.
   * Listing: The first/One/A further/Another/Yet another benefit/plus/drawback/downside of...
   * Quantity: There are numerous/potential/clear/considerable pluses/minuses to...
   * Strength: One of the most convincing/most persuasive/strongest arguments for/against... is...
   * Support: This allows residents to save time and money.
   * Comparison: In the same way... or Similarly,/Equally,/Likewise,...
 * Conclusion: Summarize and give a personal opinion.
   * Summary: All things considered,/All in all,/On balance, there are pros and cons to...
   * Opinion: Personally, I think/feel/believe/imagine...
