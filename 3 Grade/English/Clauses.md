# English Test: Conditionals & Opinion Essay

---

## 1. Conditionals & Mixed Conditionals

| Type        | Condition Clause          | Result Clause                     | Use                                        |
| :---------- | :------------------------ | :-------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------- |
| **0**       | Present Simple            | Present Simple                    | General truths, scientific facts           |
| **1st**     | Present Simple            | will / modal + bare inf.          | Real / probable future situations          |
| **2nd**     | Past Simple *(were)*      | would / could / might + bare inf. | Imaginary / unreal present or future       |
| **3rd**     | Past Perfect *(had + V3)* | would / could / might have + V3   | Imaginary / unreal past situations         |
| **Mixed 1** | Past Simple               | would / could / might have + V3   | Present condition -> Past imaginary result |
| **Mixed 2** | Past Perfect              | would / could / might + bare inf. | Past condition -> Present imaginary result |

* Modals: *would*, *could*, *might*, and *should* can all be used in conditionals.
* Mixed 1: *If he was less famous, he would have gone out dancing with his friends more.*
* Mixed 2: *If he hadn't played the part of Harry Potter, he wouldn't be one of the richest young stars today.*

## 2. Conditional Clauses (Alternatives to "If")

* **unless**: means "if not" or "except if".
  * *I wouldn't lie to you unless it was necessary.*
* **provided / providing (that)**: creates a condition (= only if). -> 1st condition
  * *I'll lend you €10 provided you pay me back tomorrow.*
* **imagine / suppose / supposing**: asks questions about hypothetical scenarios.
  * + **Past Simple (2nd Cond.)** for present/future scenarios: *Imagine you were getting married, how would you feel?*
  * + **Past Perfect (3rd Cond.)** for past counterfactual scenarios: *Suppose you had missed the train, what would you have done?*
* **Inversion (Formal style - drop "if"):**
  * Auxiliary verbs: *were*, *had*, or *should*.
  * 1st Cond: *Should you need assistance, ...*
  * 2nd Cond: *Were I in charge, ...*
  * 3rd Cond: *Had they followed my instructions, they wouldn't have got lost.*
  * Negative inversion rule: never contract negatives (*Had they not called...* NOT *Hadn't they called...*).

## 3. Opinion Essay: Structure & Essay Phrases

* **Introduction:**
  * *To explain the reasons for my view, I will examine the issue in terms of...*
  * *It is my intention to explore this issue from the viewpoint of...*
  * *This essay will look at this question from the perspective of...*
  * *I firmly believe that...*
* **Body Paragraphs (3P: Point -> Expand -> Example):**
  * *When it comes to...*
  * *It is also important to consider this issue with regard to...*
  * *Another important angle/aspect of this issue is...*
  * *A good idea would be to...*
  * *By doing this, ...*
  * *If they do this, ...* / *If ..., then ...*
  * *For example, ...*
  * *Similarly, ...*
  * *Furthermore, ... because ...*
  * *I think that ... because ...*
  * *In my view, ...*
* **Paragraph 4 (Counterargument & Rebuttal):**
  * *It is argued that...*
  * *However, I am of the opinion/view that...*
  * *While some people say ..., I ...*
  * *Some people do not agree and feel that...*
* **Conclusion:**
  * *In conclusion, ...*
  * *To sum up, ...*
  * *All things considered, ...*
  * *All in all, ...*

---

## 4. Opinion Essay: Certainty, Causes & Collocations

* **Expressing Certainty:**
  * *Undeniably, ...* / *Undoubtedly, ...*
  * *It's undeniable that...* / *There is no doubt that...*
  * *Without (a) doubt, ...*
  * *give no reason for concern* / *give no cause for concern*
* **Describing Consequences & Results:**
  * *The result / consequence / outcome of (such a decision/choice) could be that...*
  * *This could lead to / result in...*
  * *This will result in...*
  * *As a result, ...* / *As a consequence, ...*
  * *Consequently, ...*
* **Key Collocations (Causes & Effects):**
  * *a hidden motive behind (their request)*
  * *wider implications / broader consequences (of the plan)*
  * *uncover the reasons for (the closure)*
  * *under the influence of (peers)*
  * *have a huge / massive impact on (the community)*
  * *have far-reaching consequences*
  * *exert a strong influence on (teenagers)*
  * *possible explanations for (this phenomenon)*
  * *have catastrophic consequences*
  * *be a direct result of (carelessness)*
  * *produce the desired result*
  * *produce no concrete results*
  * *the result that we want to achieve*
